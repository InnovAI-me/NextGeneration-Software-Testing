\subsection{Review Protocol}
\label{subsec:review-protocol}
This review follows the systematic literature review (SLR) process
defined by Kitchenham and Charters~\cite{kitchenham2007} -- protocol
definition, search, screening against explicit inclusion/exclusion
criteria, quality assessment, data extraction, and synthesis -- and
reports study selection following the PRISMA 2020
guidelines~\cite{page2021prisma}. Protocol design preceded execution: the
research questions, search strategy, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and
quality-assessment checklist were established in advance so
that selection decisions are auditable against a declared rule set
rather than made post hoc. Every artifact referenced below -- the
search strategy, the criteria, the study extraction tracking records, and
the bibliography -- is maintained in a structured replication archive,
ensuring that the selection and extraction process is transparent,
auditable, and reproducible.

\subsection{Research Questions}
\label{subsec:methodology-rqs}
The review is organized around four research questions, stated here
for completeness and answered in turn in the Results section:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1 (Landscape/Taxonomy):} What agentic-AI, self-adaptive, and
        autonomous testing techniques have been proposed, and how can they be
        classified across the test life cycle (design~$\rightarrow$~generation~$\rightarrow$~execution~$\rightarrow$~oracle~$\rightarrow$~maintenance/triage)?
  \item \textbf{RQ2 (Autonomy level):} What degree of autonomy do these
        techniques achieve (human-in-the-loop assistance versus fully
        autonomous agentic operation), and through what architectures
        (single-agent, multi-agent, tool-augmented large language model)?
  \item \textbf{RQ3 (Empirical evidence):} What effectiveness, efficiency, and
        industrial-adoption evidence exists (benchmarks, case studies,
        practitioner studies), and how rigorous and reproducible is that
        evidence (data contamination, benchmark leakage, generalizability)?
  \item \textbf{RQ4 (Risks and open problems):} What risks (security of
        coding/testing agents, trust and acceptance, hallucination, flaky and
        self-healing failure modes) and open research gaps remain for
        next-generation, autonomous quality engineering?
\end{itemize}
These four questions drove every subsequent methodological decision: the search string's
terminology, the inclusion/exclusion criteria's scope boundaries, and
the data-extraction parameters were each derived directly from
RQ1--RQ4 so that every extracted attribute maps onto at least one research
question. Framing the methodology around these four questions also
clarifies the review's comprehensive scope: RQ4's emphasis on emerging risk and governance
guided a dedicated synthesis of the most recent safety-relevant quality engineering literature.

\subsection{Search Strategy}
\label{subsec:search-strategy}
The search targets major digital libraries and bibliographic databases --
IEEE Xplore, the ACM Digital Library, Scopus, and Web of Science -- supplemented
by arXiv and Google Scholar for preprints, since agentic-AI testing research moves
rapidly and a substantial share of pioneering work first appears as a preprint.
The search string combines three concept facets with Boolean \texttt{AND}:
(i) a software-testing facet (\textit{``software testing''}, \textit{``test automation''},
\textit{``test generation''});
(ii) an AI/automation facet (\textit{``artificial intelligence''}, \textit{``machine learning''},
\textit{``deep learning''}, \textit{``large language model''}, \textit{``LLM''},
\textit{``reinforcement learning''}, \textit{``autonomous''}, \textit{``self-healing''}); and
(iii) a next-generation/intelligence qualifier facet (\textit{``next generation''},
\textit{``intelligent''}, \textit{``AI-driven''}, \textit{``AI-augmented''}).
Terms within a facet are joined by \texttt{OR}; the three facets are joined by
\texttt{AND}, and the string is adapted to each database's query syntax (e.g., Scopus
\texttt{TITLE-ABS-KEY(\ldots)}, Web of Science \texttt{TS=(\ldots)}).
To ensure comprehensive coverage, backward snowballing (inspecting reference lists of
included studies) and forward snowballing (identifying citing studies) were conducted
on landmark publications. The sampled literature spans publication years 2018--2026,
capturing the trajectory from early AI-assisted test generation to multi-agent,
autonomous quality engineering systems.

The corpus was developed in two rigorous phases: an initial foundational corpus of
113 primary studies capturing core generative, exploratory, and maintenance
testing techniques, followed by a targeted expansion of 36 studies dedicated to
the emerging quality engineering safety thread (adversarial security of testing agents,
oracle circularity, self-healing stability, and autonomous governance). This
yielded a comprehensive corpus of 149 primary studies that underpins the taxonomy
and empirical synthesis.

\subsection{Study Selection}
\label{subsec:study-selection}
Candidate studies were screened against predefined inclusion criteria (IC) and
exclusion criteria (EC).
The inclusion criteria require that a study be:
\begin{itemize}
  \item (IC1) a peer-reviewed journal article, conference paper, workshop paper, or substantial technical preprint;
  \item (IC2) published within the 2018--2026 time window;
  \item (IC3) primarily concerned with software-testing techniques involving AI/ML, autonomy, or automation beyond scripted record-and-replay;
  \item (IC4) written in English; and
  \item (IC5) reporting an identifiable technique, tool, framework, or empirical study, or providing a recognized conceptual roadmap relevant to autonomous quality assurance.
\end{itemize}
The exclusion criteria remove:
\begin{itemize}
  \item (EC1) duplicate publications (retaining the extended journal version where available);
  \item (EC2) non-technical opinion pieces or unverified promotional literature;
  \item (EC3) short extended abstracts and posters under four pages;
  \item (EC4) articles unavailable in full text after retrieval efforts;
  \item (EC5) studies addressing hardware testing or non-software domains exclusively; and
  \item (EC6) secondary surveys, which are analyzed separately in the related work (Section~\ref{sec:related-work}) rather than classified as primary techniques.
\end{itemize}

Study screening was conducted through a two-stage filter: initial title and abstract
screening to verify topical scope, followed by detailed full-text examination and eligibility
determination. Across the 149 included studies, full-text analysis was prioritized for
representative techniques across each category, with 31 cornerstone studies undergoing
in-depth qualitative key-point extraction to substantiate the core narrative findings.

\subsection{Quality Assessment}
\label{subsec:quality-assessment}
To appraise study rigor, studies progressing to detailed extraction were evaluated
against a five-item checklist adapted from standard software engineering SLR
guidelines~\cite{kitchenham2007}:
(1) are the research objectives clearly stated;
(2) is the study design appropriate for the stated objectives;
(3) is the evaluation methodology clearly described;
(4) are threats to validity explicitly addressed; and
(5) are findings clearly reported and supported by evidence.
Each item is scored 0 (no), 0.5 (partially), or 1 (yes), yielding a composite
quality score on a 0--5 scale. Consistent with Kitchenham and Charters' recommendations,
quality scores were used to weight the evidentiary strength of reported findings during
synthesis (particularly distinguishing exploratory tool demos from large-scale controlled
experiments or industrial field studies), rather than functioning as an arbitrary elimination filter.

\subsection{Data Extraction and Taxonomy Construction}
\label{subsec:data-extraction}
For each study, structured data were extracted across multiple standardized dimensions:
bibliographic information (title, authors, year, venue, digital object identifier),
operational context (problem addressed, software application domain, target language),
technical approach (testing phase, AI/LLM model architecture, degree of autonomy, tool integration),
evaluation characteristics (benchmark datasets, comparative baselines, metrics, fault-detection effectiveness),
and reported limitations or research gaps.

These extracted attributes directly informed the construction of this review's 18-category
taxonomy, structuring the literature across the complete testing life cycle:
surveys and secondary reviews (10 studies); unit test generation with LLMs (8);
CI failure triage and flaky-test classification (9); GUI, mobile, and web testing agents (6);
human factors -- trust and acceptance (15); industrial case studies (5);
multi-agent and agentic test generation (5); test oracles and assertions (10);
self-adaptive systems and runtime testing (4); benchmarks and data contamination (4);
mobile GUI agents (4); security of coding and testing agents (15);
agentic software-engineering foundations (3); bug reproduction and test-based issue validation (3);
API testing (3); LLM-based fuzzing (3); flaky tests and self-healing tests (11);
and mutation testing with LLMs (2).
The taxonomy was built inductively following constant-comparison principles,
grouping techniques by functional responsibility and autonomy characteristics.

\subsection{Replicability}
\label{subsec:replicability}
All protocol artifacts described in Sections~\ref{subsec:review-protocol}--\ref{subsec:data-extraction} --
the query strings, inclusion/exclusion criteria, extraction schema, study classifications,
and descriptive data tables -- are compiled into a structured replication package.
This open-science design ensures that independent researchers can audit classification
decisions, verify the descriptive statistics reported in Section~\ref{sec:results}, and
extend the review as the agentic testing literature continues to expand.
